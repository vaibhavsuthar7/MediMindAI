[56] ABSTRACT
[57] "Digitalizing Residential Communities for Transparency and Efficiency"
[58] Traditional society management carries the burden of manual record-keeping, lack of transparency in financial dealings, and communication gaps between the administration and residents. This project proposes a comprehensive ApniSociety App (Smart Society Manager)” —a mobile application designed to streamline administrative tasks and improve the living experience of society members.
[59] The system features a role-based access control where administrators can manage finances, including monthly maintenance tracking and automated event-cost splitting. For residents, it offers a digital Report Area to log household or common area complaints directly to the admin.
[60] A standout feature of this application is the Smart Parking Module. It includes a vehicle directory for instant owner identification via number plates and a unique Parking Slot Sharing system. This allows residents to temporarily share their vacant parking slots with others when away, optimizing limited space. By integrating features like automated notifications and secure society joining via unique codes, this system aims to replace outdated manual registers with a transparent, real-time digital solution.

[124] CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION
[125] Problem Statement
[126] In residential societies, managing daily operations through handwritten registers and manual records has become increasingly inefficient and error-prone. Monthly maintenance charges are often recorded inconsistently, leading to payment disputes and delayed collections. Event expenses get divided arbitrarily among members, causing confusion and unfair burden distribution. House maintenance complaints either go unnoticed or take weeks to resolve due to poor tracking. Society announcements rely on physical notices that members frequently miss. Parking management creates daily conflicts—stray vehicles block spaces, absent owners' slots remain unused, and there's no quick way to identify vehicle owners through number plates.
[127] The absence of a centralized digital system forces society secretaries and admins to spend hours reconciling records, chasing payments, and resolving petty disputes. Members face inconvenience reporting issues, tracking dues, or accessing important updates. Temporary parking allocation for outstation members remains manual and disorganized. This fragmented approach not only wastes time but also breeds resentment among residents, undermining community harmony.
[128] The Smart Society Manager addresses these pain points by providing a unified digital platform where maintenance tracking happens automatically, event costs divide fairly, complaints get resolved systematically, announcements reach everyone instantly, and parking operates efficiently with vehicle lookup and temporary slot allocation. This solution eliminates paperwork, ensures transparency, and saves hours of manual work for society admins while empowering members with real-time access to all society information.
[129] Objective
[130] The primary objectives of developing the Smart Society Manager (SSM) are:
[131] To digitize maintenance management by automating monthly charge tracking, payment status updates, and reminders, eliminating errors from handwritten ledgers and ensuring timely collections for society admins.
[132] To streamline event expense handling through automatic cost division among participating members, transparent contribution tracking, and digital receipts—making event organization fair and hassle-free.
[133] To enable efficient complaint resolution via a dedicated report portal where house owners can submit issues with photos/descriptions, allowing admins to assign, track, and close complaints systematically.
[134] To improve communication with an instant notification panel for admin announcements about meetings, maintenance schedules, or emergencies, ensuring 100% member reach without relying on physical notices.
[135] To solve parking conflicts by maintaining a vehicle registry (number plate → owner details), enabling quick stray vehicle identification, and supporting temporary slot allocation for absent owners based on their travel duration.
[136] To provide multi-society support where users can create new societies with unique join codes requiring admin approval, allowing the app to scale across multiple residential communities securely.
[137] Purpose
[138] The purpose of developing the Smart Society Manager(SSM) is to create a simple yet powerful digital solution that replaces outdated handwritten registers and manual processes in residential societies. This web/mobile application centralizes all key operations—maintenance billing, event expense sharing, house complaint tracking, admin notifications, and parking management—into one user-friendly platform accessible to both society admins and members.
[139] By automating monthly maintenance updates with payment reminders, fairly dividing event costs among participants, providing a structured complaint system with photo uploads, enabling instant announcements that reach every member, and introducing smart parking features like vehicle lookup by number plate and temporary slot allocation, SMS eliminates common pain points that cause disputes and wasted time.
[140] Ultimately, this project delivers a secure, scalable system supporting multiple societies through unique join codes and admin approvals, saving administrators hours of paperwork weekly while empowering residents with real-time transparency and control over their society affairs—transforming chaotic management into organized, harmonious community living.

[142] CHAPTER 2: PROJECT SCOPE
[143] The Smart Society Manager(SSM) project focuses on developing a web/mobile application that digitizes core residential society operations for up to 100 members per society, supporting multiple societies through secure user management. This includes automated monthly maintenance charge tracking with payment status and reminders, event expense posting with automatic per-member cost division and contribution tracking, a complaint portal for house owners to report issues (with photo/text uploads and admin assignment/status updates), an admin notification panel for instant announcements about meetings or emergencies, and a parking module for vehicle registration (number plate to owner mobile/name lookup), stray vehicle detection, and temporary slot allocation based on owner absence duration.

[147] Basic dashboard showing dues, upcoming events, personal complaints, and vehicle status.
[148] Simple reports/export for maintenance and event expenses (CSV/PDF).
[149] Responsive UI for mobile/desktop access.
[150] Exclusions (Out of Scope):
[151] Actual payment gateway integration (only tracks payment status).
[152] Advanced accounting/audit trails or GST compliance.
[153] Hardware integration (CCTV for parking, physical access control).

[267] CHAPTER 5: PROCESS MODEL
[268] Incremental Model
[269] The Incremental Model is chosen for the development of the Smart society manager(SSM). This model combines the structured approach of linear development with the flexibility of iterative enhancement by building the system in smaller, manageable increments. Each increment adds functional components to an evolving system.
[270] The decision to adopt the Incremental Model is based on the modular nature of the project requirements: features such as User Authentication, Medical Report Upload & Analysis, Doctor Finder Integration, Session History, and Admin Management can be independently developed, tested, and improved in successive increments. This approach facilitates early delivery of usable functionality, inviting timely user feedback and enabling risk management.
[271] The main stages of the Incremental Model applied for SSM include:
[272] Requirement Analysis: Clear identification and documentation of functional and non-functional requirements for each increment, laying a solid foundation for development.
[273] Design & Development: Detailed design and coding of individual modules or features within each increment, ensuring they align with overall system architecture.
[274] Testing: Each increment undergoes comprehensive testing including unit testing, integration testing, and user acceptance testing to validate functionality and performance.
[275] Implementation: Successful increments are integrated into the main system and deployed, providing users with incremental improvements and new features.
[276] This model supports rapid delivery of critical features early in the project lifecycle and is flexible to evolving needs, fostering continuous improvement and user-centric development throughout the HIA system’s creation
